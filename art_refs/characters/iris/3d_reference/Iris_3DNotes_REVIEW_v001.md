## Identity lock

- Preserve the long, straight deep-plum hair and the silver observer band; do not shorten the hair into a generic bob or replace the band with decorative animal ears.
- Preserve the cold-white asymmetric split long coat over the ink bodysuit, with a clearly adult feminine chest-waist-hip rhythm.
- Preserve the near-height folding energy observation lance as the primary silhouette feature, plus the monocular visor and compact range module as secondary features.
- Keep crimson as a small targeting accent and compressed emission color. It must not become a broad red costume mass.
- The equipment is fictional observation technology. Do not turn it into a replica of a real firearm or copy recognizable external-IP weapon construction.

## Expand, delete, combine

- Expand the head, sharp adult eyes, hands, boots, visor aim point, lance grip, fold hinge and the two coat-fork tips for gameplay-camera readability.
- Delete micro seams, tiny straps, redundant scope noise, unsupported observation modules and the 2D long-leg fashion exaggeration.
- Combine the coat tails into two major physics panels, the range devices into one readable hip cluster, and the telescoping lance into three major blocks.
- Keep the feminine waistline visible even when the coat and lance overlap the torso; avoid replacing the figure with a straight armored column.

## Motion, physics and camera

- Physics candidates: the lower third of the hair and the two coat panels only. Clamp both away from the face, visor, shoulders and lance grip.
- The silver observer band, visor, range cluster, lance body and its folding joints are rigid attachments, not cloth or hair physics.
- Provide deployed, folded and stowed lance states. The stowed state must keep the near-height weapon outside the central gameplay-camera silhouette.
- Validate coat clearance at shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee and ankle extremes before adding secondary motion.
